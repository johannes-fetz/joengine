# 3D without glitches on the Saturn

Applies to every 3D content: a model converted from OBJ (`jo_model.py`), a model built by a Python program
(`jo_mesh.py`), a mesh built in C, a terrain, a level.

## The three causes of almost every glitch

1. **No z-buffer.** The VDP1 draws the polygons in the order of a painter's sort with **one depth per polygon**. Two
   polygons that overlap with close depths flicker or cut each other. Build the model so that one drawing order is
   always right.
2. **The whole texture is mapped on the quad**, without perspective and without texture coordinates. Its orientation
   comes only from the order of the 4 corners (A: top left of the texture, B: top right, C: bottom right, D: bottom
   left) and from the flip flags.
3. **The gouraud doesn't saturate**: a channel that goes over 31 wraps around to dark.

## Geometry

- **Quads, not triangles.** A triangle is a quad with 2 corners at the same place (A, B, C, C): its texture is squeezed
  toward a corner and its sort is worse. An unavoidable triangle: untextured, gouraud only. A folded untextured quad (a
  far terrain on a slope) can be cut into 2 triangles along its diagonal; a textured folded or twisted quad gets another
  row of points instead.
- **No polygon laid on another one** (a decal, a plate, a coplanar quad): same depth, it flickers. Paint the detail in the
  texture of the face under it. A real relief stands out clearly.
- **Nothing inside a closed volume**: a hidden polygon ends up sorted in front of the face that covers it.
- **Don't model what is never seen** (a wall between two buildings that touch, the bottom of an object on the ground):
  it costs memory and CPU, and badly sorted it shows through.
- **No double sided faces by convenience** (`jo_dual 1`, `JO_3D_MATERIAL_DUAL_PLANE`): drawn from the back too, it comes
  in front of what it should leave visible. A part seen from both sides: two single faces, or a small closed box.
- **Cut the big faces** next to small objects: a big wall sorted on its center comes in front of an object against it.
- **No twisted quad** (a corner far out of the plane of the 3 others): the VDP1 draws it in lines from the edge A-D to
  the edge B-C, and seen from some angles it becomes a bow tie with a missing part.
- **Parts that stick out of a volume** (wheels, mirrors): a real gap of a few centimeters, single faces, sorted on their
  nearest point.
- **A big textured quad that can cross the near plane** (a sign near a road): cut it in the model, each piece with its
  part of the texture; one piece cut by the near plane squeezes the whole texture into what remains.
- **A quad made into a triangle at run time** (2 corners at the same place): its 3 first corners must be distinct (A B C
  C), else the side of the quad on the screen (the culling) is wrong.
- **The camera never films a thin quad very close or on its edge**: keep it away from the walls (the engine cuts at the
  near plane, but a face bigger than the screen near the camera costs a lot of VDP1 time).

## Sorts (material flags, MTL keys of `jo_model.py`)

- Default: sorted on the **center** of the polygon.
- `jo_sort max` (`JO_3D_MATERIAL_SORT_MAX`, the farthest point): what must go behind (floors, terrain, a dark inside
  behind windows).
- `jo_sort min` (`JO_3D_MATERIAL_SORT_MIN`, the nearest point): what must go in front of what it touches (shadows on the
  ground, the sides of the wheels, panels a few centimeters off a face).
- Without `jo_sort`, `jo_model.py` chooses the sort of each polygon by itself (views all around the model drawn like the
  VDP1 and with a z-buffer): use it, then check.
- To put a whole model in front of or behind another one (a vehicle on the ground, its shadow): a depth bias
  (`jo_3d_set_depth_bias()`).
- **A sort key below 0 is set to 0**: several negative keys become equal and their order is lost. Keep the computed keys
  above a floor.
- Details on a sloped surface (lights, vents, plates): paint them in the texture of the surface; a quad laid on it
  shows and hides with the angle.
- An object seen through a transparent surface (under water, behind a window in screen doors): split it into small
  parts, each one with its own bias computed from where the line of sight crosses the surface; the water module
  (`water.h`, `jo_water_add_model()`) does it for the objects under its sea.
- Many small parts each with its bias: one small mesh per part drawn whole costs less than ranges of a big mesh.

## Orientation and axes

- Each quad is **clockwise seen from its front** (Saturn axes: X right, Y down, Z forward). A face is turned over by
  reversing the order of its corners; a cyclic rotation doesn't turn it over.
- Swapping corners 1 and 3 transposes the texture: swap by pairs (0-1 and 2-3) and flip horizontally instead.
- The engine culls a single sided face by the order of its corners on the screen; a non flat quad is kept as soon as one
  of its 2 triangles faces the camera. A check in Python must do the same, not test the normal.
- With Y down, going up is `dy < 0`. Angles in `jo_angle`, from `jo_atan2_angle()`.
- **Never reason alone about a sign or an orientation: test it** (force a rotation by a test option, capture, read).

## Textures

- Width multiple of 8. Color 0 is transparent. In 16 colors, index 15 is an end code (two of them stop the line): 14
  useful colors (`jo_model.py` and `jo_mesh.py` do it). No end codes on a texture flipped horizontally.
- **Unique texture names**: the engine shares the textures by name between all the models.
- **Detail at the scale of the texel**: compute the size of a texel on the screen in the nearest case. Fine contrasted
  details become blotches; the large scale variation must dominate. Look at each texture at x4, x1 and x1/4 in 16 colors.
- **Edge textures carry their direction** (earth at the foot, grass at the top): the direction on the screen depends on
  the order of the corners and on the side; check it with a gradient texture (red at x = 0, yellow at x = max), captured
  from both sides at several places.
- Don't combine a horizontal flip and the reduced copies (mipmaps) on the same texture: make a mirrored copy of the
  texture instead.
- One textured face = one quad: two quads end to end with the same texture show it twice. If a face must be cut, give
  each piece its part of the texture.

## Colors, gouraud, light, transparency

- **Keep the gouraud at most 1.0** (16 of 31) on light textures; to light a paint up, darken it in the texture first.
- A game that writes the gouraud tables or the colors of a model at run time: load it between
  `jo_3d_set_unique_gouraud_tables(true)` and `(false)` (one table per polygon), and without the uniform gouraud
  optimization (`jo_3d_set_uniform_gouraud_skipped(false)`, see `Documentation/performance.md`).
- Palettes of the VDP2 (`jo_create_palette()`): the color k of the data is shown by the pixel k + 1 (the index 0 is
  transparent), like the TGA loader.
- No light that depends on the heading or on the camera on a model that turns: tables written in two halves over two
  frames make polygons flicker. A fixed light in the space of the model.
- Big transparent surfaces (windows, water, shadows): **screen doors**, never the VDP1 half transparency (it reads the
  frame buffer: slow, and stripes). Behind a screen doors window: a dark closed inside, else the scenery shows through.

## Levels of detail, scenery, terrain

- **Change the level of detail with the distance on the ground**, not with the depth in the view (else the mesh changes
  when the camera turns).
- **Measure the distance to the object**, not to its bounding sphere when the sphere is very asymmetric.
- **The same points at the borders of all the levels of detail**: no T-junction between neighbor pieces or between
  levels (cracks). Two neighbor quads touch without a crack only if they share their corners.
- A reduced level that merges several materials keeps the material of the longest part, and the point where the
  material changes stays.
- Far scenery: an image on a VDP2 plane (mountains, sky), not far 3D tiles.
- Objects put on the ground (trees, people, signs on posts): on the **drawn** ground, with their foot on it; never on a
  slope where the band of terrain in front of them is sorted over their foot; the whole width of a sprite in front of a
  wall, not only its foot.
- **Standing sprites (trees)**: drawn with the proportions of their image (`jo_sprite_get_width()`,
  `jo_sprite_get_height()`), not too close to the path of the camera, a few species and sizes.
- **A terrain in bands along a path never shows the sky** if: a band is never removed on a slice and kept on the next
  one; a band goes up to the crest on a rising slope; the rows of two samples don't cross (inside the turns); what the
  bands don't cover is filled by small tiles of a height field, tested on their whole surface.
- **A model drawn over a big area** (a piece of track with its hills): its culling uses the spheres of its parts, not one
  sphere for everything.
- Every change of geometry is measured: images per second, polygons per frame, memory, VRAM.

## Loading another level during the game

- **The sprites and textures are a stack** (`jo_sprite_free_from()`, `jo_3d_model_free_textures_from()` free everything
  from an id): load what changes (the level) last, what stays before it.
- Free everything allocated for a level with the free of each module (`jo_3d_free_model()`, `jo_3d_free_mesh()`,
  `jo_water_free()`, `jo_water_free_dip()`, `jo_level_free()`, `jo_cells_free()`, `jo_entities_free()`), then its textures
  (`jo_3d_model_free_textures_from()`), and the tables and the states that point into it. Check with several round trips
  that the free memory comes back and doesn't fragment.
- A model shown only in a menu: keep it compressed in RAM (`jo_lz.py`), create it only while the menu is shown.

## Performance of the 3D

- Count the polygons drawn per category (level of detail, scenery, characters) before optimizing.
- **One object, one draw call**: an object drawn as one mesh (`jo_3d_mesh_draw()`, a model with `jo_3d_model_draw()`)
  costs about 0.6 of the same quads drawn one by one (`jo_3d_draw()`, `jo_3d_draw_array()`, the cube of
  `jo_3d_create_cube()`): each call transforms its points and prepares its drawing. Build the objects as meshes
  (`jo_3d_create_mesh_from_vertices()`) or as JOM models (their points shared by several quads are transformed once).
- **Sprites facing the camera** (particles, items, trees): `jo_3d_draw_billboard()` or `jo_3d_draw_standing_sprite()`
  cost about a third of `jo_3d_draw_sprite()` (a quad that goes through the polygon pipeline).
- The cost per polygon of the pipeline (transformation, projection, sort, command) is the main CPU cost; the polygons out
  of the screen cost almost as much as the visible ones in its loop: culling by parts and draw distances are the levers.
- A polygon near the camera is drawn in parts (texture subdivision): each part costs about one polygon. Subdivide only
  what is near and big on the screen.
- Moving the drawing of objects to the slave CPU gains only if their jobs read little shared data: measure both CPUs and
  the time the master CPU waits.
- `JO_MIN()` and `JO_MAX()` evaluate their arguments twice: put an expensive call in a variable first.
- Depth of many points of an object: transform the origin and the 3 axes once, then 3 products per point.

## Checks before showing a model

1. `python3 Compiler/COMMON/jo_model.py --check-sort MODEL.JOM` (views all around: the painter's sort of the VDP1
   against a z-buffer, and the holes). A model built in Python: `jo_mesh.Mesh.sort_scene()`.
2. Automatic checks in the generator that stop the build: orientation of the closed volumes, overlapping quads, twisted
   quads, a terrain rendered with the levels of detail of the game (a sky pixel under ground pixels is a hole). A check
   uses **the same rules as the game** (culling, level of detail, distances), and it must fail on a known defect first.
3. **Labeled captures in the emulator**: the position (and the direction) written on the screen, around the model, near
   and far, two captures very close in time (a polygon that changes = a flicker). Zoom x3 before concluding.
4. Look around in steps of 45 degrees at many places of a level, not only along the path of the camera: a hole behind
   the camera of the game is seen as soon as the player turns.
5. Before touching the engine for a glitch, read the VDP1 command table of a save state at the place of the defect: a
   glitch is often a duplicated or extra polygon in the data, not the engine.
6. Keep everything generated: the generator and its sources are kept, the files of `cd/` are written by it, never edited
   by hand.
7. After each change of geometry or textures: polygons, memory at loading, VDP1 VRAM use (keep a margin), images per
   second in the heaviest scene.
